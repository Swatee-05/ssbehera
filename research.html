2. Quantum Bit Error Rate (QBER) & Eavesdroppers

In the BB84 Defense game, Eve attempts to intercept photons using a randomly chosen basis. According to quantum collapse principles, when Eve measures in the wrong basis, she alters the photon state with a $50\%$ probability. This introduces detectable errors in the key stream, driving up the Quantum Bit Error Rate (QBER).

If the QBER exceeds the threshold of 25%, Alice and Bob abort the protocol, recognizing that the channel is no longer secure.

Applications: What Can You Do After Quantum Keys Are Generated?

  • Information-Theoretic Security (One-Time Pad): Combining generated keys with plaintext using XOR encryption yields mathematically unbreakable communication.
  • Instant Tamper Detection: Eavesdropping attempts inevitably introduce quantum error noise, giving Alice and Bob immediate notification of intrusions.
  • Post-Quantum Infrastructure Protection: Protects critical power grids, financial transactions, and military networks against future quantum supercomputers.
